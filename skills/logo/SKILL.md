# logo

A logo here is a small piece of geometry drawn by a script, plus a name set in a real typeface and converted to outlines, locked together and exported from one `brand.json`. Claude designs in SVG directly. It never generates a raster image and traces it: that gives pictures, not logos, and they fall apart at 16 px.

## The idea in three lines

1. **One idea, drawn simply.** A logo carries a single idea (what the thing is, does or feels like), built from a few shapes on a grid. If it needs explaining, it's an illustration. Use `cover-art` or `linewash` for those.
2. **Small first.** It has to survive 16 px, one colour and a dark background before it is allowed to be pretty at 512.
3. **Choose type by eye.** Set the actual name in many real typefaces on one sheet and pick from that, not from a font's reputation.

## Where the work lives, and its license (required)

Everything this skill makes for a project goes in one folder named `brand/` (in a repo, at its root; outside one, `<name>-brand/`), with its own license. Never leave logos, rounds or the kit loose in the project, and never let them fall under the code's license.

```
brand/
  LICENSE          all rights reserved; not covered by the code license
  TRADEMARKS.md    the usage policy
  draw.mjs, brand.json, *.svg   the source
  rounds/01/, rounds/02/        every round of sketches
  dist/            the export (brand.json's "out")
```

- **The default is exclusion.** The name, logos and brand material are all rights reserved and excluded from the project's code license, whatever that is. An MIT or BSD license would otherwise let anyone reuse the logo as freely as the code, and only Apache 2.0 (section 6) and a few others hold trademarks back on their own. Only an owner who says so in words gets a different license for the brand folder (for example CC BY for a community project); then replace `brand/LICENSE` and say what changed.
- **`export.mjs` writes both files** into the export (so the zip carries them) and at the top of `brand/` when they're missing, from `brand.json`'s `legal` (owner, year, contact, code license; all have defaults, and the code license is read from the repo's own LICENSE). It leaves existing ones alone, so an owner's edits stay. Fix any warning it prints.
- **`TRADEMARKS.md` is short and plain.** Allowed without asking: talking about the project by name, saying a project works with or is built on it, linking with the unmodified logo, keeping the files unchanged in a fork. Not allowed: a fork, product or service under the name or a confusable one, modified logos, implied endorsement. Keep it to what fits that project.
- **Say it at the root.** In a repo, add one line to the root README's license section: "The code is <license>. The <Name> name and logos, and everything in `brand/` (and its copies in `public/`), are not; see `brand/LICENSE` and `brand/TRADEMARKS.md`." List any other place the logos are copied to. Leave the root LICENSE file's text alone, so GitHub still recognises it.
- These files are a sensible default, not legal advice; say so once when delivering, next to the note that the logo hasn't been checked for trademark conflicts.

## Workflow

1. **Brief.** Read the project first if there is one (README, landing page, existing colours and tokens, any current logo). Then write the brief in six lines: *name*, *what it does in one sentence*, *who it's for*, *three to five adjectives*, *colours to keep or avoid*, *the category's clichés* (the lightbulb, the globe, the speech bubble, the leaf, the generic "S" swoosh). Add a one-line category audit: how five to eight competitors' logos look (colours, shapes, type), so the concepts can stand apart. Ask only what you can't find. If the user is away, go with your best reading and say what you assumed.
2. **Direction.** Ask the five direction questions in `references/direction.md` in one message: three feeling words, four either/ors, reference brands, where the logo lives most, and weirdness from 1 (quiet) to 5 (weird). Each has a default, so don't wait on an answer; with no weirdness given, the grid's columns are levels 2, 3 and 4. The answers steer the concepts, the type voices and the colour (the file has the table). In the same message, ask which parts of the kit they want (see "Choosing the exports" below), with all of them as the default.
3. **Concepts.** Write fifteen or more one-line concepts (twenty or more at weirdness 5), from **different metaphors and different structures**: symbols, lettermarks, monograms (always one when the name has two words or the brand goes by initials; `references/monograms.md`), object letters (a letter built from objects in the brand's world, when the name points to one; `references/object-letters.md`), wordmarks with one modified letter or a letter replaced by an object (pictorial wordmarks, same file), negative-space marks (`references/negative-space.md`), abstract marks, emblems and badges (`references/emblems.md`). If the full logo will have detail that dies at small sizes, plan its responsive ladder (`references/responsive.md`). For each, note how it will be built: on the 256 grid with straight cuts, or on a circle grid (`references/circle-grid.md`), which suits anything round, organic or figurative that should look engineered. Describing what the product *does* gives a wider spread than prescribing a shape. Choose nine for a 3 × 3 grid: rows are three structures (by default symbol, lettermark or monogram, wordmark; swap a row for an object letter when the name points to a world, or for an emblem or abstract mark if the brief points there), columns are three weirdness levels (2, 3 and 4 when no level was given; otherwise the chosen level and its neighbours, such as 4, 5, 5 at a 5). No two cells share a metaphor.
4. **Type.** Pick two or three voices from `typefaces.json` that match the adjectives and the weirdness level and run `specimen.mjs` on the name, adding any extra faces with `--font`. Try the name in sentence case and in capitals or lower case if it might suit. Shortlist two or three faces, and note any letter with a shape the mark could borrow, such as a round `o`, a shared stem or a notch. The sketches use the front-runner; the shortlist stays open until round 2.
5. **Sketch round (round 1).** Draw all nine in black only, one script, one SVG per cell, as `examples/wren/round-1/draw.mjs` does. Sketches are quick but honest: the idea must be readable and the silhouette must hold at 32 px, but no colour, lockups, optical polish or construction sheets yet. Lay them out with `node scripts/render.mjs 1.svg … 9.svg --sheet round-1.png --mono --rows "Symbol,Lettermark,Wordmark" --cols "Weirdness 2,Weirdness 3,Weirdness 4" --title "<Name> · round 1 · nine unrefined sketches, pick one to three"`, which numbers them 1 to 9 and shows each at 32 and 16 px and reversed. Look at the small ones first and replace any cell that's mush.
6. **Pick, then draw properly (round 2), then lock.** Show the grid and the specimen sheet, calling them unrefined sketches, with a one-line note per cell and your recommendation (say which cells are risky and what they give up). **Stop and wait for the user to pick one to three by number;** this and the lock are the user's decisions, so never skip them, even if they're away: leave the sheet and wait. Then draw each pick properly: three silhouettes and two refining passes for a creature or object (craft.md, "Creatures and objects"), colour, the horizontal lockup in the shortlisted face, a `constructionSheet()` for a circle-grid mark, a 32 px check first for a monogram. Show them side by side at 512 and 32 px. Iterate by number ("2, but a lighter wordmark") until the user says this is the logo. Keep every round in `brand/rounds/01/`, `brand/rounds/02/` so nothing is lost. **Nothing else in the kit is made until the logo is locked**, and it's then made once, with no variations.
7. **Refine** the locked logo against `references/craft.md`: optical corrections, consistent radii and stroke weights, tracking, a simplified favicon if the mark has a detail under 1/32 of its size, and dark and one-colour versions. Draw the chosen concept five or more ways (proportions, weights, crops) and pick again; the best details are often found, not planned. For a circle-grid mark, write `constructionOverlay()` and set `logos.mark.construction` so the guidelines show the circles. Write `brand.json`, including `direction` (the answers and the chosen weirdness), `about` (two short paragraphs for the guidelines' introduction), `construction` (one sentence on how the mark is built), a `usage` line per colour, and for the templates `website`, `contact` (a real person, or leave the placeholders) and `templates.background` (the field colour, when the primary is too close to black).
8. **Portfolio art** (when `portfolio` is in the exports; read `references/portfolio.md`). Write the one verb the product does and three one-line scene concepts from different patterns, pick one and say why. Draw it from one script as `brand/portfolio/cover.svg` (2400 × 800) and `cover-mobile.svg` (1000 × 800, the same scene re-framed), with the subject in the middle 60 %, the outer 40 px quiet, its own background and no words; write the alt text; add `portfolio` to `brand.json`. Run `node scripts/portfolio.mjs brand.json`, read `portfolio/preview.png` and fix what fails before the full export.
9. **Export and check.** Run `node scripts/export.mjs brand.json` (it prints which exports it's making), then read `dist/check.png` and whichever of these the chosen exports made: every `dist/brand-guidelines-N.png` (three pages, one more for the palette and up to two for the templates), `dist/web/palette.png` (the export lists any colour pair below WCAG AA), `dist/social/og-image.png`, `dist/portfolio/preview.png`, and the template previews (`templates/email/preview.png`, `templates/business-card/preview.png`, `templates/slides/slide-N.png`, `templates/social/*.png`) (the export warns if a page overflows Letter). Fix whatever fails the checklist below and export again. Expect at least two passes.
10. **Deliver** two ways at once: the files people preview (attach `check.png`, the main logo SVG, and, when they were made, `brand-guidelines.pdf`, `social/og-image.png`, `portfolio/preview.png` with the alt text, and the template previews to the reply) and the one download that has everything (`<name>-brand-kit.zip`, which opens to the kit's `README.md`). Keep the draw script and `brand.json` with it in `brand/`, so the logo can be changed later by editing code. In a repo, everything stays in `brand/` with its `LICENSE` and `TRADEMARKS.md`; the logo SVGs are copied where the site keeps its images and `icons/` into `public/` with `head.html` pasted into the layout, and the root README gets the line saying the brand isn't under the code license (see "Where the work lives"). Say plainly that the logo hasn't been checked for trademark conflicts and that the license files aren't legal advice.

## Choosing the exports

Not everyone needs everything: a code library may want icons and a palette but no business card or slides. So ask, once, which parts of the kit to make, and make all of them unless the user picks fewer. Show the menu from `node scripts/exports.mjs`:

1. Icons: favicons, app and PWA icons, `site.webmanifest`, `head.html`
2. Brand guidelines: the printable PDF
3. Website palette: CSS, Tailwind and design tokens with a contrast report
4. Social images: link preview and avatar
5. Email signature
6. Business card
7. Slide template
8. Social post templates
9. Portfolio art: a wide and a phone cover drawn for the project, a square icon and alt text, for a case-study page and Ko-fi

and say what is always included: the logo files (SVG, PNG and PDF, with dark and one-colour versions), `check.png`, the kit's `README.md`, the brand's `LICENSE` and `TRADEMARKS.md`, and the zip. The core can't be turned off: it's the logo itself, and the license files are required (see "Where the work lives").

- **Ask with the direction questions** (step 2), so the answer is in hand by the export and nothing waits on it. If it's never answered, make everything. When the skill is only exporting (a refresh, or a kit for a logo that exists), ask before the first export instead, with the same default.
- **Record the answer** in `brand.json` as `"exports"`: `"all"` or a list of ids (`icons`, `guidelines`, `palette`, `social`, `email`, `card`, `slides`, `posts`, `portfolio`; `templates` stands for the four templates). `node scripts/exports.mjs "1, 3, slides"` prints the line to paste. `--exports` on `export.mjs` overrides it for one run.
- **Changing your mind is cheap.** Edit the list and export again: parts added are made, parts dropped are removed from `dist/`, so the zip holds only what was chosen. The guidelines show only the pages for parts that were made.
- Portfolio art is the one export that needs drawing beyond the logo: a scene for the covers. A refresh or a kit for an existing logo can still make it (step 8 needs only the locked logo and its colours). Leave it out for a project that will never have a case-study page.
- Leaving out icons drops the favicon and app-icon checks from `check.png`; say so, and suggest keeping icons for anything with a website or app.

## Style rules (the short version; details in craft.md)

These are the rules at weirdness 1 and 2. From 3 up, `references/direction.md` says which ones may bend (asymmetry, then off-baseline letters and overlaps, then any of them). The review checklist below never bends.

- **Geometry** Build from circles, arcs, rectangles and polygons with numbers you chose: a 256 grid, integer or half-unit coordinates, angles of 0, 15, 30, 45, 60 or 90°, one corner radius and one stroke width per logo. Strokes are outlined: everything is a filled path, holes use `fill-rule="evenodd"`, and no shape is painted in the background colour to fake a hole.
- **Economy** Three to five shapes, one or two colours plus an optional accent, no gradients, filters, masks, shadows or `<text>`. Anything thinner than about 1/32 of the mark's height disappears at favicon size.
- **Type** One family for the wordmark; a second only for body text. Set it, then adjust it: tighten or loosen tracking, and modify at most one letter where the idea lives. Never fake bold, italic or condensed by stretching.
- **Lockups** The wordmark's cap height is about 40–55 % of the mark's height in a horizontal lockup, with a gap of about a third of the mark. The stacked version centres the word under the mark. Both use the same mark and word, just placed differently.
- **Colour** Start from the product's own tokens if it has them. One primary colour that passes 4.5:1 on white (it will carry the wordmark), an accent used on one part of the mark, and a paper and a dark. Every version must also work in pure black and pure white. The website palette grows from these four, so pick the paper and dark as the real page colours (a near-white and a near-black) and let the export make the rest.

## Review checklist (read check.png and the brand-guidelines pages)

- At 16 and 32 px the mark is still recognisably itself, not a smudge; the favicon reads in both the light and the dark tab.
- A mark that depicts something passes the silhouette test: solid black at 64 px, it can be named in one word. Its parts join up or are clearly apart, and the part that carries the idea isn't the smallest thing in it (see craft.md, "Creatures and objects").
- One-colour black, white on the ink, and white on the accent all hold, with no part relying on a second colour to be seen.
- The wordmark's spacing looks even (round letters a touch closer, no holes around `r`, `y`, `T`, `A`); the lockup looks balanced, with neither half shouting.
- The mark sits optically centred in the app icon and inside the circle on the maskable icon; the iOS icon has no transparency.
- Turned 90°, 180° and 270°, and mirrored, it makes no accidental letter, symbol or rude shape (monograms especially).
- Blurred until it's a soft shape (or seen across a room), each concept still has a recognisable silhouette and its key negative space; the one that survives the most blur is the strongest.
- Construction claims are true: "golden ratio" only if `series()` built it, "circle grid" only if `grid.check()` passes.
- No near-misses: edges that almost align, curves that almost meet, two radii or stroke widths that are nearly but not quite equal.
- Nothing in it is a cliché from the brief, and it isn't a near copy of a well-known logo. If it reminds you of one, change it.
- The SVGs have `viewBox`, a `<title>`, no `<text>`, no embedded raster, and each is under ~10 KB (wordmarks under ~25 KB).

## Templates

The export also makes the pieces every brand needs next, all from `brand.json` and following `references/templates.md`: an email signature, a business card, a slide template and social posts. They're samples with placeholder copy that says what goes where, so the person can use them directly or hand them to whoever makes the next thing. Each is one of the exports (`email`, `card`, `slides`, `posts`), so the user can take any of them or none. For a template the export doesn't make (a letterhead, a poster, merch), follow the same shared rules: one field colour, the logo version that matches the ground, the brand's two faces, and the accent once.
