# logo

A logo here is a small piece of geometry drawn by a script, plus a name set in a real typeface and converted to outlines, locked together and exported from one `brand.json`. Claude designs in SVG directly. It never generates a raster image and traces it: that gives pictures, not logos, and they fall apart at 16 px.

## The idea in three lines

1. **One idea, drawn simply.** A logo carries a single idea (what the thing is, does or feels like), built from a few shapes on a grid. If it needs explaining, it's an illustration. Use `cover-art` or `linewash` for those.
2. **Small first.** It has to survive 16 px, one colour and a dark background before it is allowed to be pretty at 512.
3. **Choose type by eye.** Set the actual name in many real typefaces on one sheet and pick from that, not from a font's reputation.

## Files

Paths are relative to this skill's folder.

| File | Use |
|---|---|
| `scripts/brand.mjs` | The kit. `loadFont(family, weight)` fetches a Google Font once (cached in `~/.cache/brand-kit`). `text(font, str, {size, tracking, x, y, anchor})` returns the word as an outlined path with its measurements. `lockup({mark, font, name, layout})` builds horizontal or stacked lockups. Shape helpers: `circle`, `ellipse`, `ring`, `rect`, `poly`, `ngon`, `polar`, `arcBand`, `place`, and for creatures and objects `smooth` (one flowing outline through points, with sharp corners where marked) and `limb` (a round-ended stroke for legs, stalks and handles), plus `svg()` and `write()`. Import it from a draw script by absolute path; never hand-type a logo's SVG. |
| `scripts/geometry.mjs` | Construction tools. Booleans on paths (`union`, `subtract`, `intersect`, `exclude`, `dilate`, `erode`, `outline`), circle grids (`circleGrid(fib(8, 6))` records every circle; `grid.c`, `grid.touch`, `grid.fillet`, `through`, `meet`, `grid.check()`), the construction drawings (`constructionSheet`, `constructionOverlay`), and monogram tools (`solid` for glyphs, `bar`, `polyline`, `weave`, `knockout`). Uses paper.js, installed once into the cache like opentype.js. |
| `scripts/specimen.mjs` | `node scripts/specimen.mjs "Name" --group geometric,humanist [--font "Fraunces:600"] [--out DIR]` sets the name in every listed face at matched cap height on one numbered sheet (`specimen.svg` + `.png`). |

The scripts need Node 18+. Rendering uses Playwright's Chromium if one is installed; exit 2 means there's no browser, so deliver the SVGs and say the PNGs couldn't be made. Fonts and the dependencies (`opentype.js`, and `paper.js` for `geometry.mjs`, each installed once into the cache folder) need network access to `fonts.googleapis.com`, `fonts.gstatic.com` and the npm registry.

## Workflow

1. **Brief.** Read the project first if there is one (README, landing page, existing colours and tokens, any current logo). Then write the brief in six lines: *name*, *what it does in one sentence*, *who it's for*, *three to five adjectives*, *colours to keep or avoid*, *the category's clichés* (the lightbulb, the globe, the speech bubble, the leaf, the generic "S" swoosh). Add a one-line category audit: how five to eight competitors' logos look (colours, shapes, type), so the concepts can stand apart. Ask only what you can't find. If the user is away, go with your best reading and say what you assumed.
2. **Direction.** Ask the five direction questions in `references/direction.md` in one message: three feeling words, four either/ors, reference brands, where the logo lives most, and weirdness from 1 (quiet) to 5 (weird). Each has a default, so don't wait on an answer; with no weirdness given, the grid's columns are levels 2, 3 and 4. The answers steer the concepts, the type voices and the colour (the file has the table).
3. **Concepts.** Write fifteen or more one-line concepts (twenty or more at weirdness 5), from **different metaphors and different structures**: symbols, lettermarks, monograms (always one when the name has two words or the brand goes by initials; `references/monograms.md`), wordmarks with one modified letter, abstract marks, emblems. For each, note how it will be built: on the 256 grid with straight cuts, or on a circle grid (`references/circle-grid.md`), which suits anything round, organic or figurative that should look engineered. Describing what the product *does* gives a wider spread than prescribing a shape. Choose nine for a 3 × 3 grid: rows are three structures (by default symbol, lettermark or monogram, wordmark; swap a row for an emblem or abstract mark if the brief points there), columns are three weirdness levels (2, 3 and 4 when no level was given; otherwise the chosen level and its neighbours, such as 4, 5, 5 at a 5). No two cells share a metaphor.
4. **Type.** Pick two or three voices from `typefaces.json` that match the adjectives and the weirdness level and run `specimen.mjs` on the name, adding any extra faces with `--font`. Try the name in sentence case and in capitals or lower case if it might suit. Shortlist two or three faces, and note any letter with a shape the mark could borrow, such as a round `o`, a shared stem or a notch. The sketches use the front-runner; the shortlist stays open until round 2.
5. **Sketch round (round 1).** Draw all nine in black only, one script, one SVG per cell, as `examples/wren/round-1/draw.mjs` does. Sketches are quick but honest: the idea must be readable and the silhouette must hold at 32 px, but no colour, lockups, optical polish or construction sheets yet. Lay them out with `node scripts/render.mjs 1.svg … 9.svg --sheet round-1.png --mono --rows "Symbol,Lettermark,Wordmark" --cols "Weirdness 2,Weirdness 3,Weirdness 4" --title "<Name> · round 1 · nine unrefined sketches, pick one to three"`, which numbers them 1 to 9 and shows each at 32 and 16 px and reversed. Look at the small ones first and replace any cell that's mush.
6. **Pick, then draw properly (round 2), then lock.** Show the grid and the specimen sheet, calling them unrefined sketches, with a one-line note per cell and your recommendation (say which cells are risky and what they give up). **Stop and wait for the user to pick one to three by number;** this and the lock are the user's decisions, so never skip them, even if they're away: leave the sheet and wait. Then draw each pick properly: three silhouettes and two refining passes for a creature or object (craft.md, "Creatures and objects"), colour, the horizontal lockup in the shortlisted face, a `constructionSheet()` for a circle-grid mark, a 32 px check first for a monogram. Show them side by side at 512 and 32 px. Iterate by number ("2, but a lighter wordmark") until the user says this is the logo. Keep every round in `rounds/01/`, `rounds/02/` so nothing is lost. **Nothing else in the kit is made until the logo is locked**, and it's then made once, with no variations.
7. **Refine** the locked logo against `references/craft.md`: optical corrections, consistent radii and stroke weights, tracking, a simplified favicon if the mark has a detail under 1/32 of its size, and dark and one-colour versions. Draw the chosen concept five or more ways (proportions, weights, crops) and pick again; the best details are often found, not planned. For a circle-grid mark, write `constructionOverlay()` and set `logos.mark.construction` so the guidelines show the circles. Write `brand.json`, including `direction` (the answers and the chosen weirdness), `about` (two short paragraphs for the guidelines' introduction), `construction` (one sentence on how the mark is built), a `usage` line per colour, and for the templates `website`, `contact` (a real person, or leave the placeholders) and `templates.background` (the field colour, when the primary is too close to black).
8. **Export and check.** Run `node scripts/export.mjs brand.json`, then read `dist/check.png`, every `dist/brand-guidelines-N.png` (six pages with templates), `dist/web/palette.png` (the export lists any colour pair below WCAG AA), `dist/social/og-image.png`, and the template previews (`templates/email/preview.png`, `templates/business-card/preview.png`, `templates/slides/slide-N.png`, `templates/social/*.png`) (the export warns if a page overflows Letter). Fix whatever fails the checklist below and export again. Expect at least two passes.
9. **Deliver** two ways at once: the files people preview (attach `brand-guidelines.pdf`, `check.png`, the main logo SVG, `social/og-image.png` and the template previews to the reply) and the one download that has everything (`<name>-brand-kit.zip`, which opens to the kit's `README.md`). Keep the draw script and `brand.json` with it, so the logo can be changed later by editing code. In a repo, the logo SVGs go where the site keeps its images and `icons/` goes in `public/` with `head.html` pasted into the layout. Say plainly that the logo hasn't been checked for trademark conflicts.

## Style rules (the short version; details in craft.md)

These are the rules at weirdness 1 and 2. From 3 up, `references/direction.md` says which ones may bend (asymmetry, then off-baseline letters and overlaps, then any of them). The review checklist below never bends.

- **Geometry** Build from circles, arcs, rectangles and polygons with numbers you chose: a 256 grid, integer or half-unit coordinates, angles of 0, 15, 30, 45, 60 or 90°, one corner radius and one stroke width per logo. Strokes are outlined: everything is a filled path, holes use `fill-rule="evenodd"`, and no shape is painted in the background colour to fake a hole.
- **Economy** Three to five shapes, one or two colours plus an optional accent, no gradients, filters, masks, shadows or `<text>`. Anything thinner than about 1/32 of the mark's height disappears at favicon size.
- **Type** One family for the wordmark; a second only for body text. Set it, then adjust it: tighten or loosen tracking, and modify at most one letter where the idea lives. Never fake bold, italic or condensed by stretching.
- **Lockups** The wordmark's cap height is about 40–55 % of the mark's height in a horizontal lockup, with a gap of about a third of the mark. The stacked version centres the word under the mark. Both use the same mark and word, just placed differently.
- **Colour** Start from the product's own tokens if it has them. One primary colour that passes 4.5:1 on white (it will carry the wordmark), an accent used on one part of the mark, and a paper and a dark. Every version must also work in pure black and pure white. The website palette grows from these four, so pick the paper and dark as the real page colours (a near-white and a near-black) and let the export make the rest.

## Review checklist (read check.png and the brand-guidelines pages)

- At 16 and 32 px the mark is still recognisably itself, not a smudge; the favicon reads in both the light and the dark tab.
- A mark that depicts something passes the silhouette test: solid black at 64 px, it can be named in one word. Its parts join up or are clearly apart, and the part that carries the idea isn't the smallest thing in it (see craft.md, "Creatures and objects").
- One-colour black, white on the ink, and white on the accent all hold, with no part relying on a second colour to be seen.
- The wordmark's spacing looks even (round letters a touch closer, no holes around `r`, `y`, `T`, `A`); the lockup looks balanced, with neither half shouting.
- The mark sits optically centred in the app icon and inside the circle on the maskable icon; the iOS icon has no transparency.
- Turned 90°, 180° and 270°, and mirrored, it makes no accidental letter, symbol or rude shape (monograms especially).
- Blurred until it's a soft shape (or seen across a room), each concept still has a recognisable silhouette and its key negative space; the one that survives the most blur is the strongest.
- Construction claims are true: "golden ratio" only if `series()` built it, "circle grid" only if `grid.check()` passes.
- No near-misses: edges that almost align, curves that almost meet, two radii or stroke widths that are nearly but not quite equal.
- Nothing in it is a cliché from the brief, and it isn't a near copy of a well-known logo. If it reminds you of one, change it.
- The SVGs have `viewBox`, a `<title>`, no `<text>`, no embedded raster, and each is under ~10 KB (wordmarks under ~25 KB).

## Templates

The export also makes the pieces every brand needs next, all from `brand.json` and following `references/templates.md`: an email signature, a business card, a slide template and social posts. They're samples with placeholder copy that says what goes where, so the person can use them directly or hand them to whoever makes the next thing. Set `"templates": false` to skip them. For a template the export doesn't make (a letterhead, a poster, merch), follow the same shared rules: one field colour, the logo version that matches the ground, the brand's two faces, and the accent once.
